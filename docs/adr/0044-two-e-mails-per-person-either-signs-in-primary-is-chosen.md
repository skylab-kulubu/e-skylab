# A person has a school e-mail and a personal e-mail; either signs in; the person chooses the primary

Keycloak stores a single `email`, but a YTÜ member has an address proven by the YTÜ Microsoft login and usually a personal one. We decided that the **School e-mail** (`schoolEmail` attribute, `school_email` claim) is immutable and set only by the YTÜ link, a **Personal e-mail** is added by the person and proven by a code mailed to it, and the **Primary e-mail** is whichever of the two the person chooses; the primary is what Keycloak `email`, tokens, core and SkyMail see. Password login accepts username, primary e-mail or school e-mail through a SKY LAB authenticator, so both addresses always work. A **Verified YTÜ account** cannot change its name or school e-mail; an unverified account can edit its name and may link its YTÜ account later, after which the lock applies.

Rejected: keeping the personal address as a second attribute that every service must learn (SkyMail, core, Forms would all need a second address); forcing the school address to stay primary (people want club mail where they read it); allowing a self-declared, unverified address to become primary.

_Update (2026-09-22): the proof is a six-digit code, not a link. The person reads it from the mailbox and types it into Account center while signed in as themselves; the code is checked only against that person's own pending change (one per person, so a second tab or device works too); it lives ten minutes and dies after five wrong attempts. A link that works without a session let anyone who could make SkyMail mail a person also get that person to attach their address to someone else's account by clicking; a code only works for the account that requested it. A link bound to a session would have failed on a phone that is not signed in to `my.`, which is where most people open mail._
